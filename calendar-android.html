Show your plan in Google Calendar

Android

Unpact can publish your accepted day plans as a calendar you subscribe to, so your planned blocks appear alongside your meetings. It is one way: your plan goes out, and nothing you change in Google Calendar comes back.

The awkward part first, so it is not a surprise. The Google Calendar app on Android cannot add a subscribed calendar. There is no button for it. Subscriptions are added on the web at calendar.google.com and then sync down to your phone — or you use a small helper app that can do it on the device. Both routes are below.

First, get your link

  • Open Unpact and go to Profile (the gear icon).
  • Under Calendar, tap Copy the link instead.
  • Send it somewhere you can reach it later — email it to yourself, or save it to Keep. It is a long link starting https://api.unpact.app/api/calendar/.

Treat that link like a password. Anyone who has it can read the titles and times of your planned blocks. If you ever share it by accident, tap Replace the link in the same place — the old one stops working straight away.

Option 1 — Google Calendar on the web

No extra app, but you need a browser view of the full site. A computer is easiest. On the phone you can use Chrome with Desktop site turned on, because the mobile version of Google Calendar hides the settings.

  • Go to calendar.google.com and sign in.
  • In the left sidebar find Other calendars and click the + beside it.
  • Choose From URL.
  • Paste your Unpact link and click Add calendar.
  • It appears in the sidebar under Other calendars.

Then do this, or it will not show on your phone. A calendar added on the web is often not switched on for the Android app. Open the Google Calendar app, tap the menu (three lines), go to Settings, find the new Unpact calendar in the list, and turn Sync on. This is the step people miss when the calendar appears on their computer but never on their phone.

One limitation worth knowing: Google decides how often it re-reads a subscribed calendar, and it is not quick — changes can take many hours to appear. It is fine for a plan you made the night before. It is not going to reflect a block you moved five minutes ago.

Option 2 — a helper app, if you want it to update faster

If the delay in Option 1 bothers you, an app called ICSx⁵ (free, on the Play Store) exists to do exactly this job. It subscribes to a calendar link on the phone itself, lets you choose how often it refreshes, and the result shows up in Google Calendar like any other calendar.

  • Install ICSx⁵ from the Play Store.
  • Open it and tap + to add a subscription.
  • Paste your Unpact link and continue.
  • Give it a name, pick a colour, and choose a sync interval.
  • Grant it calendar permission when asked. Your Unpact blocks then appear in Google Calendar.

We are not affiliated with ICSx⁵ — it is simply the usual answer to a gap Android leaves open.

If nothing appears

  • Have you accepted a plan? Only accepted plans are published. A draft you have not accepted stays on your phone.
  • Give it a refresh cycle. Nothing is instant here — see the note in Option 1.
  • Check the calendar is switched on in the Google Calendar app's settings.
  • Check the link. It should end in .ics. If you replaced it after subscribing, the old subscription is dead and you need to add the new link.

On iPhone

Simpler: tap Show my plan in another calendar in Profile and iOS offers to subscribe straight away. No link to copy and no settings to visit.
